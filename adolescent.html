Risk Behaviour & Health Problems Among Rural Adolescents

Cumulative findings from three cross-sectional surveys of 1,018 adolescents aged 10–19, carried out during Residential Field Site Training (RFST) in five villages of Dhamrai and Saturia upazilas.

These findings describe respondents in a few purposively sampled villages. They are not a national prevalence estimate.

Why adolescence matters

10–19

Adolescents in Bangladesh

5 aims

What the surveys set out to do

Measure risk behaviours, relate them to social and economic context, identify common health problems, assess hygiene and health knowledge, and find out where treatment is sought.

Focus on drug use

Reported use is low. Interpret with care.

Taking, inhaling or injecting a substance regularly or occasionally. Small numbers: differences between surveys are not a confirmed time trend.

2.3%

23 of 1,018 reported use.

Asked about: ganja, phensidyl, sleeping pills, glue (dandi), alcohol, heroin or pethidine, yaba and chewing tobacco.

Substances named

25 mentions from 23 respondents. No one reported yaba or glue inhalation.

Reasons given

21 responses. Reasons were not recorded for every respondent.

Why 2.3% may be an underestimate

A taboo subject

Survey teams reported that social norms discouraged adults and adolescents from discussing drugs.

Peer influence

Smoking answers point to pressure and imitation among friends, suggesting a related social influence.

A limited sample

Face-to-face interviews in a few purposively sampled villages may miss some experiences.

What the evidence says

01

The constraints are connected. Education gaps, economic barriers, poor hygiene and risk-taking affect adolescent development.

02

Knowledge is uneven. More than half had not heard of STDs or AIDS.

03

Drug use requires careful interpretation. 2.3% reported use, mainly cannabis and alcohol; stigma may lead to under-reporting.

04

An integrated response is needed. Health education and life-skills support should work together.

Five practical priorities

01

Community care

Make community-based health care available and affordable.

02

Hygiene education

Teach safe drinking water, sanitation and personal hygiene.

03

STD awareness

Run dedicated education programmes on sexually transmitted diseases.

04

Smoking & drugs

Increase awareness of smoking and substance-related harm.

05

Parent counselling

Support parents and relatives as adolescents grow.

Limitations

Small, purposive sample

A few villages, so results may not represent all rural adolescents in Bangladesh.

Sensitive topics

Social norms limited questions about sex, drugs and weapons, which may have affected disclosure.

Short field visits

Time constraints and unforeseen conditions limited data collection.

Data notes. RFST 2014 (n = 360), RFST 2016 A (n = 300), RFST 2016 B (n = 358); pooled n = 1,018 unless stated otherwise. Pooled percentages are weighted by group sample size. Population figures are from BBS 2011.
